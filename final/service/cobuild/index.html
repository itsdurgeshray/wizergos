Challenges

Enterprise solution delivery lifecycle

A Structured Lifecycle from Initiation to Continuous Improvement

Wizergos solution delivery can be organized into connected stages: Governance and Initiation; Solution Design and Planning; Build; Quality Assurance; User Acceptance Testing; Deployment and Release Management; and Post-Production Support.

Each stage has defined responsibilities and outputs, helping project, business, development, QA, UAT, DevOps and support teams coordinate their work.

Support

Manages production incidents, root-cause analysis, monitoring, knowledge-base updates and enhancement requests.

Integrations and connectivity

Connect Enterprise Applications and Services

Enterprise solutions often need to exchange information with upstream and downstream applications. The delivery process includes joint planning of integration endpoints, authentication methods, request and response formats, and error handling.

Integration methods described in the delivery framework include REST, SOAP, GraphQL and batch endpoints. Authentication options are finalized with the relevant application teams based on project requirements.

Ongoing support and enhancements

Keep Improving After Go-Live

Solution delivery continues after deployment. Support teams manage production issues, incident tickets, service-level-based resolutions, root-cause analysis and performance monitoring. Development teams support stabilization and provide patches or hotfixes where required.

New requests are logged, assessed for impact, categorized, planned into a release cycle, developed, regression-tested, validated through UAT and deployed through a controlled release process.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What stages are covered in the solution delivery process?

The framework covers governance and initiation, solution design and planning, build, QA, UAT, deployment and release management, and post-production support.

Which teams are involved?

Depending on the project, teams may include project management, business analysis, development, external integration partners, QA, UAT, DevOps and support.

What low-code components are referenced?

The framework references forms, workflows, rules, bots, integrations, dashboards and reporting components.

Can the solution connect with external applications?

Integration requirements are planned jointly with relevant application teams, including endpoints, authentication, data formats and error handling.

What happens when new requirements arise?

Enhancement requests are logged and assessed for impact, then planned, developed, tested, validated and released through a controlled process.

Can the client take over solution management?

A client-handover model is described as an optional ownership approach. The exact handover scope should be agreed for each engagement.

Is training provided before handover?

The framework includes training and knowledge transfer covering low-code builders, integrations, monitoring, release management and solution documentation.
